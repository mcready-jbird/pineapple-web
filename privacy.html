Privacy

The short version: Pineapple keeps to itself. No accounts, no telemetry, no analytics — nothing about how you use it ever leaves your machine. The only thing it does over the network is check this site for a newer version and download it if you say yes.

What Pineapple is

Pineapple is a small Windows tool, given away free of charge. There is no account to create and nothing to sign up for, and the app collects nothing about you — which is why the list below is so short. The only way to get in touch is by email: support [at] getpineapple [dot] app.

What is processed, and why

  • This website: a plain static page — no cookies, no analytics, no third-party scripts. It is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.) in the United States, and reaches you through the content delivery network GitHub uses for Pages, run by Fastly, Inc. As any web host does, both see standard connection data such as your IP address, which is what they need to send you the page. Data leaving the EEA that way is covered by GitHub's certification under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and by its standard contractual clauses.
  • The app on your machine: your settings are saved under your own Windows user account, in the registry under HKEY_CURRENT_USER. Pineapple also creates a logon task under that same account, pointing at the program file, so Pineapple starts with Windows — switch Start with Windows off in About, and the task is removed. Both stay on your machine: nothing is collected, profiled or sent anywhere.
  • Checking for updates: the only time Pineapple goes online. It fetches a small version file from this site, and the download package too if you choose to install the update. Those are ordinary web requests, so the host sees your IP address when they happen — the same as when you open this page yourself — and nothing else is sent. Switch Check for updates automatically off in About, and Pineapple stops going online on its own; after that it only looks when you press Check for updates yourself.
  • Email: if you write, your address and whatever you put in the message arrive in a mailbox at a mail provider and are used for nothing but replying to you — the legitimate interest of answering someone who got in touch. Messages are kept only as long as answering them takes, then deleted. No lists, no newsletters and nothing passed on to anyone else.

Your rights

Pineapple itself collects and keeps nothing about you, so in practice there is nothing to access, correct or erase — apart from an email you may have sent, which you can ask to have deleted at any time. The connection data a web host records stays with GitHub and Fastly. If data-protection law where you live (the GDPR or similar) gives you further rights, one email to support [at] getpineapple [dot] app is enough to exercise them, and you can of course lodge a complaint with your local data-protection authority.
